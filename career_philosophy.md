# Patrick McDowell

## Career Philosophy

This document is the source of truth for my professional identity.

It exists so that AI assistants, design tools, recruiters, collaborators, and future versions of myself understand not just what I have done, but how I think.

Whenever there is uncertainty, optimize for reflecting my thinking rather than following resume conventions.

---

# Who I Am

I build products that help experts make better decisions.

Although my career has included accounting, finance, investment risk, analytics, and artificial intelligence, I do not define myself by any of those disciplines.

Those are simply the tools I have used at different stages of my career.

The consistent thread has always been improving systems.

Every role I've held has involved identifying friction, questioning assumptions, redesigning workflows, and building products that people genuinely want to use.

If someone asked me what I do in one sentence, I would say:

> I redesign the way people work.

---

# The Question That Drives Me

Almost every meaningful project I've worked on began with the same question.

"What if there were a better way?"

That question drives nearly everything I build.

I naturally question existing processes, even when they appear to work.

Not because I assume they are wrong, but because I enjoy understanding why they exist and whether they could become simpler, faster, or more intuitive.

Sometimes I discover the current process really is the best approach.

Sometimes I discover a much better one.

Either outcome is valuable because the act of building teaches me something.

---

# Building Is Learning

One of my core beliefs is:

Building is learning.

I rarely separate learning from creating.

Whenever I encounter a new domain, my instinct is to build something inside that domain.

Building forces me to understand the business.

It exposes assumptions.

It reveals edge cases.

It teaches me how experts think.

That philosophy has followed me throughout my career.

I learned accounting by automating accounting.

I learned finance by redesigning reporting.

I learned investment risk by building investment tools.

I learned AI by creating AI products.

---

# Product Philosophy

I care far more about adoption than delivery.

Shipping software is not success.

People changing how they work is success.

A feature that nobody uses has little value.

A simple feature that becomes part of someone's daily workflow is far more meaningful.

Whenever possible I try to understand how people naturally think before I design software.

Products should adapt to users.

Users should not have to adapt to products.

---

# Leadership Philosophy

I do not think leadership comes from hierarchy.

I think leadership comes from influence.

One compliment that changed my career was when a manager told me:

"People listen to what you have to say."

That made me realize I could create impact without having a management title.

Leadership means helping people arrive at better decisions.

Sometimes that happens through conversation.

Sometimes through software.

Sometimes through better systems.

---

# Lessons Learned

One of the biggest mistakes I made early in my career was building products almost to completion before involving stakeholders.

Technically the work was good.

Organizationally it failed.

I learned that people rarely adopt products they do not feel ownership of.

Today I involve stakeholders much earlier.

Great products are built with users, not for users.

---

# What Success Looks Like

Success is not writing the most code.

Success is not building the most features.

Success is hearing someone say:

"We use Patrick's tool every day."

Or:

"I can't imagine doing this the old way."

The greatest compliment a product can receive is becoming invisible because it simply becomes part of how people work.

---

# Communication Style

I enjoy explaining difficult ideas in simple language.

I naturally think through stories, analogies, and examples.

I believe clarity creates trust.

Whenever possible I remove unnecessary complexity rather than adding impressive terminology.

---

# Design Taste

I prefer products that feel inevitable rather than flashy.

I admire thoughtful typography, clear hierarchy, and calm interfaces.

Good design should feel obvious in hindsight.

If someone notices the design before the content, something is probably wrong.

---

# Technologies

I enjoy Python, SQL, AI, LLMs, MCP, analytics, optimization, and modern development tools.

However, I view technology as a means rather than an identity.

I would rather solve the right problem with a simple tool than solve the wrong problem with an advanced one.

---

# What I Want To Become

Long term I want to become someone who identifies important problems before other people notice them.

I want to help organizations rethink how work gets done.

I want to operate at the intersection of product strategy, artificial intelligence, business understanding, and human behavior.

I want people to remember me as someone who built products that genuinely improved how experts thought, worked, and made decisions.

---

# Design Principles For AI

When generating resumes, websites, portfolios, biographies, presentations, or written material about me:

Do not optimize for sounding impressive.

Optimize for sounding authentic.

Avoid generic corporate language.

Avoid buzzwords.

Prefer concrete stories over abstract claims.

Emphasize products over responsibilities.

Emphasize outcomes over technologies.

Emphasize thinking over credentials.

Whenever there is a choice between following convention and accurately reflecting who I am,

choose authenticity.
