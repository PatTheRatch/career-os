# My Philosophy on AI

AI is not replacing human intelligence.

It is changing where human intelligence creates value.

For most of my career, my advantage came from writing better SQL, better Python, better Tableau dashboards, and better automation.

Today those skills are becoming increasingly accessible.

That excites me.

Because I never wanted to be valuable because I could type code faster.

I wanted to be valuable because I could recognize problems worth solving.

AI shifts more of my attention toward understanding people, identifying opportunities, designing systems, and making better decisions.

That is where I believe human value is moving.

---

## Building is Thinking

I don't build software because I enjoy writing code.

I build software because building is one of the fastest ways to understand a problem.

Every prototype teaches me something.

Every failed idea eliminates another path.

Every product changes how I think.

AI dramatically reduces the cost of experimentation.

Instead of spending two weeks testing an idea, I can spend an afternoon.

That means I can learn faster.

The real productivity gain isn't writing code.

It's accelerating understanding.

---

## AI Should Expand Human Capability

I am rarely interested in replacing experts.

I'm interested in making experts dramatically more effective.

The best AI systems don't remove people from the process.

They remove unnecessary friction.

A portfolio manager should spend more time thinking about investments.

An analyst should spend more time analyzing.

A designer should spend more time designing.

Good AI quietly eliminates everything else.

---

## Product Before Technology

People often begin with:

"What can AI do?"

I prefer to begin with:

"What frustrates people today?"

Only then do I ask whether AI is the right tool.

Sometimes it is.

Sometimes it isn't.

Technology should follow the problem.

Not the other way around.

---

## My Goal

I don't want to build AI products.

I want to build products that become better because they use AI.

That's an important distinction.

People remember products that improve their work.

They rarely remember which model powered them.
